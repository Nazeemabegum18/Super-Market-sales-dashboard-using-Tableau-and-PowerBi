# **SuperMarket Sales**

This project is all about exploring sales and shipping data through interactive dashboards built in Tableau and Power BI. Using the popular Superstore dataset from Kaggle (2011–2014), I wanted to see how data visualisation can help a supply chain manager quickly spot trends in sales, profit, and shipping costs across different regions and customer segments.

## Dataset

The dataset originally had around 51k records, but I sampled it down to 3k rows after cleaning, making it easier to work with for visualization.
Key information includes:

1. Sales, Profit, Quantity
2. Shipping Cost and Mode
3. Product Category & Sub-category
4. Market, Region, and Year
5. Customer Segments

## What I Built
I designed a set of dashboards with different visualisation styles:

  - Choropleth Map – sales profit by country
  - Packed Bubble Chart – sales volumes across the top 3 markets by customer segment
  - Stacked Bar Chart – sub-category sales among top markets
  - Tree Map – average shipping costs across regions and ship modes
  - Interactive Dashboards – with filters for year, market, and customer type

## Tools Used

**Tableau** – main tool for building dashboards and exploring visual storytelling.

**Power BI** – used alongside Tableau to test differences in design and flexibility (with some DAX for calculations).

## Key Findings

  - The United States dominated sales, contributing the largest share of revenue, while some regions in Asia and Europe showed strong growth potential.
  - Technology products generated the highest profit margins, whereas Furniture often had lower profitability despite steady sales.
  - Standard Class shipping was the most frequently used mode, but Same Day shipping drove higher costs per order.
  - Consumer segment placed the most orders, but Corporate clients contributed significantly to total revenue through bulk, high-value purchases.
  - Seasonal trends were visible — Q4 (holiday season) consistently showed sales spikes across multiple regions.
  - Profitability wasn’t always linked to sales volume: some high-revenue categories had thin margins due to high shipping or discount costs.

## Conclusion

This project demonstrated how interactive dashboards can transform the Superstore dataset into actionable business insights. By visualising sales, profit, shipping, and customer behaviour across different regions, it became clear where the business performs strongly and where challenges lie. Tableau offered flexibility in building complex, multi-layered dashboards, while Power BI provided a more straightforward approach for mapping and quick reporting.
Overall, the analysis highlighted the importance of data visualisation in supporting decision-making. A supply chain manager or business analyst could use these dashboards to identify profitable categories, optimise shipping strategies, and better understand customer segments. This project was not just about creating visuals, but about telling a story with data.
